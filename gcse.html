Practical and calculation skills for every topic

Identify the independent variable you change, the dependent variable you measure and the control variables you keep constant. Explain how each control makes the comparison fair. Choose a useful range and enough readings to reveal a trend, then repeat to assess variation.

Random errors cause scatter; repeated readings and a mean can reduce their effect. A systematic error shifts results consistently and is not removed by averaging. Check zero readings and calibration. Resolution is the smallest change an instrument displays; greater resolution alone does not guarantee accuracy.

Label graph axes with quantities and units. Use a sensible scale and draw a best-fit line or curve, not a dot-to-dot line. Calculate a gradient using a large triangle on the best-fit line. A straight line shows a linear relationship; direct proportionality also requires it to pass through the origin. Investigate anomalous results before excluding them.

Convert units before substitution: milli means 10⁻³, kilo means 10³, and micro means 10⁻⁶. Square or cube conversion factors for areas and volumes. Show the equation, rearrangement, substituted values and final unit. Keep extra digits during working and round the final answer to sensible precision.

When evaluating a method, name a specific source of uncertainty, explain its effect and suggest a practical improvement. “Repeat it” addresses random scatter, not every problem. Identify hazards and explain how the chosen control reduces the risk. These notes support, rather than replace, supervised practical work.

Physics or Combined Science: what changes?

Comparison of AQA 8463 and the physics component of AQA 8464
FeatureGCSE Physics · 8463Combined Science: Trilogy · 8464
QualificationOne GCSE in PhysicsTwo GCSEs covering biology, chemistry and physics together
Physics examsTwo papers, each 1 hour 45 minutes and 100 marks; each worth 50% of PhysicsTwo physics papers within six science papers; each 1 hour 15 minutes and 70 marks, worth 16.7% of the full qualification
Paper 1Energy, electricity, particle model of matter and atomic structure
Paper 2Forces, waves, magnetism and electromagnetism, plus space physicsForces, waves, magnetism and electromagnetism; no space physics topic
Additional contentExtra material within shared topics, as well as space physics. Questions are individually labelled Physics only where applicable.Shared content only. A shared topic title does not mean every separate Physics subtopic is included.
TierBoth offer Foundation and Higher. Higher-only content is distinct from Physics-only content: some Higher material belongs to both courses.
Required practicalsIncludes extra investigations into thermal insulation and light reflection/refraction.Follow the Trilogy practical requirements; use its specification rather than the separate Physics practical numbering.

Paper 2 can also draw on earlier energy and electricity ideas. Practical skills, equations, units and interpreting data matter throughout both courses.
